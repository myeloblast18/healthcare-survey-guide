Physician survey guide

Paid medical surveys for physicians: how to choose legitimate panels

A concise guide for doctors, residents and fellows evaluating compensated healthcare market-research surveys.

Why physicians are recruited for paid surveys

Healthcare companies and research organizations seek clinicians' perspectives on treatment patterns, product concepts, medical devices, health technology, practice workflow and other topics. A research panel acts as an intermediary: it verifies members, identifies studies that match a physician's profile and sends invitations when the physician may qualify.

What to expect

Credential verification

Healthcare-specific panels may confirm identity, licensure or other professional information. Verification helps ensure that research responses come from the intended clinical audience.

Screening questions

A study may need a narrow participant group based on specialty, setting, prescribing experience or another criterion. Passing a site's general verification does not guarantee eligibility for every study.

Compensation varies

Honoraria can differ by study length, research method, specialty and recruiting difficulty. Look for compensation information provided before you commit substantial time.

Panels physicians commonly encounter

M3 Global Research, Sermo, All Global Circle, MDForLives and ZoomRx all publicly describe healthcare market-research or paid medical survey opportunities. See the full comparison for first-party sources.

Before joining

  • Use a unique password and verify that you are on the company's real domain.
  • Read the panel's privacy terms and payment rules.
  • Never disclose identifiable patient information.
  • Answer screeners accurately; inconsistent responses can undermine research quality or panel eligibility.
  • Keep a simple record of completed studies and payments for your own records.

Specialty-specific searches: An oncologist, cardiologist, primary-care physician, psychiatrist or surgeon may see very different invitation volume because each research project recruits its own target sample. For that reason, this guide does not label one company as universally “highest paying.”
