Paid medical surveys for oncologists and hematologists

Oncology and hematology market research may ask about treatment sequencing, biomarker testing, supportive care, clinical workflows, and emerging therapies. Demand changes by disease area, geography, practice type, and recruiting quota. This guide helps specialists compare panels rather than promising a particular income.

Referral disclosure: Registration links below may compensate this guide. Compensation and invitations are not guaranteed. Panel screening rules and offers can change.

What to compare before signing up

  1. Specialty targeting: Check whether a panel recruits your specific clinical practice or subspecialty.
  2. Time cost: A short screener that repeatedly disqualifies you can make an attractive honorarium misleading.
  3. Compensation details: Confirm honorarium, payment method, timing, and country eligibility before completing studies.
  4. Professional verification: Use legitimate provider websites and avoid disclosing identifiable patient information.

Questions oncologists should ask

Are survey invitations available every week?

Not necessarily. Research panels invite participants when a project matches their professional profile and quota.

Can fellows participate?

Some study opportunities may be available to trainees, but training stage and clinical experience requirements vary. Review each invitation.

Which is the highest paying panel?

No single platform can be identified reliably for every subspecialty. Compare your actual accepted invitations, screen-out time and payment experience.
