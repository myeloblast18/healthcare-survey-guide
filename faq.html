Healthcare survey FAQ

Paid medical survey questions, answered

Straightforward answers about legitimacy, eligibility, verification, screening, compensation and referral links.

Are paid medical surveys legitimate?

Yes. Healthcare market research is a real industry, and established panels recruit qualified healthcare professionals for compensated studies. Legitimacy does not mean every website using the phrase “medical survey” is trustworthy, so verify the domain, review company terms and avoid unrealistic guaranteed-earnings claims.

Who can take paid healthcare surveys?

Eligibility varies. Some studies recruit physicians; others recruit nurses, NPs, PAs, pharmacists, residents, fellows, dentists, administrators, allied health professionals, patients or caregivers. Each research project can have its own sample criteria.

Why do panels ask to verify credentials?

Researchers often need responses from a precisely defined professional audience. Healthcare-specific panels may verify identity or professional credentials to improve research integrity and reduce fraudulent participation.

Why am I screened out of a survey?

A survey may need a very specific population. A screener checks whether your professional experience matches the current study. Being screened out does not necessarily mean anything is wrong with your panel account.

How much do paid medical surveys pay?

There is no single reliable rate across the industry. Compensation depends on the company, study type, length, specialty, geography and recruiting difficulty. Because rates change and invitation volume is unpredictable, this directory does not publish an invented “average income” figure.

Do referral links change my compensation?

Referral programs are company-specific. A referral may compensate the person who referred you under the panel's current terms. When this site uses a referral link, we will disclose it clearly. Check the company's current referral terms for any effect on sign-up bonuses or other promotions.

What information should I never put in a market-research survey?

Do not disclose patient-identifiable information. Follow your professional obligations, employer policies and the study's instructions. If a question appears to request information you should not disclose, stop and contact the research company.

Is this site itself a survey company?

No. Healthcare Survey Guide is an independent directory. It helps healthcare professionals compare research panels and then links to the companies' own sites for registration and current terms.
